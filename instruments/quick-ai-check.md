# HCOS™ Quick AI Check

**A Rapid Human-Centered Review of AI, Automation, Capacity, and Human Outcomes**

**Version:** 1.0 Draft
**Framework:** Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)
**Instrument Type:** Rapid Assessment
**Parent Architecture:** HCOS™ Human-Centered AI Architecture
**Status:** Draft / Under Development

---

## 1. Purpose

The HCOS™ Quick AI Check is a short-form version of the HCOS™ Human-Centered AI Assessment.

Use it when there is not enough time for the full instrument but a decision still needs a human-centered review.

It is designed for:

* team meetings,
* design reviews,
* procurement discussions,
* workflow changes,
* AI pilots,
* policy discussions,
* implementation decisions,
* or early-stage AI proposals.

The Quick AI Check asks:

> **What should remain human, what should AI carry, who receives the benefit, and what happens to the humans involved?**

---

# 2. The 12-Question HCOS™ Quick AI Check

## 1. Human Need

> **What human problem are we actually trying to solve?**

Do not begin with:

**What can AI do?**

Begin with:

**What does the human need?**

---

## 2. Human Value

> **What part of this activity do humans value being part of?**

Ask whether the value is primarily in:

* the result,
* the process,
* the relationship,
* the learning,
* the creativity,
* the judgment,
* or some combination.

Remember:

> **Not all human effort is load. Some effort is where meaning lives.**

---

## 3. Human Load

> **What unnecessary burden is the human carrying?**

Look for:

* repetitive administration,
* memory burden,
* navigation,
* documentation,
* coordination,
* information retrieval,
* reconciliation,
* or other system friction.

Then ask:

> **Can the burden be eliminated before we automate it?**

---

## 4. Capacity Protection

> **What human capacity could be lost if AI carries this?**

Consider:

* judgment,
* learning,
* creativity,
* autonomy,
* relationships,
* communication,
* confidence,
* or meaningful skill.

Ask:

> **Are we removing burden—or removing something humans still need or value being capable of doing?**

---

## 5. Intelligence Allocation

> **Who should carry what?**

Choose:

### Human-Led

Humans retain primary judgment or participation.

### AI-Led

AI carries repetitive, administrative, computational, retrieval-heavy, or otherwise unwanted work.

### Shared

AI expands human capability while humans retain meaningful judgment, participation, and authority.

Ask:

> **Which allocation creates the greatest human value?**

---

## 6. Responsibility

> **Who is actually in control, and who is held responsible?**

Ask:

* Who can override the AI?
* Who has final decision authority?
* Who remains accountable?
* Does the responsible human understand enough to intervene?

Remember:

> **Responsibility should follow functional control.**

---

## 7. Capacity Return

> **If AI saves time, energy, attention, or effort, who gets it back?**

Ask whether the gain becomes:

* recovery,
* better quality,
* more human connection,
* learning,
* creativity,
* more sustainable output,
* or simply more workload.

Then ask:

> **Did AI create more human capacity—or merely more available output?**

---

## 8. Optimization Alignment

> **What is the system actually optimizing?**

Identify:

**Human Purpose → Metric → Incentive → AI Target**

Then ask:

> **If we become extremely good at optimizing this target, will humans actually be better off?**

---

## 9. Proxy Check

> **Has the metric started replacing the purpose?**

Examples:

**Learning ≠ Test Score**

**Connection ≠ Engagement**

**Health ≠ Quality Metric**

**Human Value ≠ Productivity**

Remember:

> **Do not mistake the score for the thing that matters.**

---

## 10. Empowerment

> **Does the human have meaningful power in their relationship with the technology?**

Can they:

* access it,
* understand it,
* control relevant parts,
* benefit from it,
* decline it where appropriate,
* challenge a consequential decision,
* correct errors,
* and obtain meaningful human review?

Remember:

> **Being subject to AI is not the same as being empowered by AI.**

---

## 11. Power

> **Who gains power because of this technology?**

Ask:

* Who gains more information?
* Who gains more monitoring capability?
* Who gains speed?
* Who gains decision authority?
* Who becomes more dependent?
* Who can challenge the system?

Then ask:

> **If the person with less power disagrees, what happens to them?**

---

## 12. Human Outcomes

> **What actually happened to the humans?**

Look at:

* dignity,
* autonomy,
* agency,
* capacity,
* human load,
* connection,
* safety,
* understanding,
* learning,
* creativity,
* access,
* sustainability,
* and flourishing.

The final test is:

> **Would we still call this AI successful if success were measured from the human side of the system?**

---

# 3. Five-Question Ultra-Quick Check

When even twelve questions are too many, ask:

## 1.

> **What does the human need?**

## 2.

> **What should remain meaningfully human?**

## 3.

> **What should AI carry?**

## 4.

> **Who gets the benefit?**

## 5.

> **What happened to the human?**

These five questions should remain visible throughout AI design and governance.

---

# 4. Red Flags

Pause for deeper review when:

* AI is being introduced mainly because it is available.
* meaningful human participation is removed without asking whether people value it.
* humans remain accountable while AI holds functional control.
* efficiency gains immediately become more workload.
* productivity improves while human capacity declines.
* the metric becomes more important than the underlying human purpose.
* humans cannot meaningfully refuse or contest AI decisions.
* human review exists only formally.
* AI increases surveillance more than it increases human capability.
* automated failure creates new burden for humans.
* an average positive result hides concentrated harm.
* technology works technically but humans appear less autonomous, less capable, or more depleted.

A red flag does not automatically mean:

**Stop the technology.**

It means:

> **Use the full HCOS™ Human-Centered AI Assessment before expanding the system.**

---

# 5. Quick Decision Categories

After completing the Quick AI Check, classify the proposal as:

## Proceed

The allocation appears human-centered and risks are manageable.

## Proceed With Safeguards

The use case is valuable but requires specific protections.

## Pilot and Learn

The human outcome remains uncertain and should be tested on a limited scale.

## Reallocate

The current human/AI division of work appears poorly designed.

## Redesign

Important human load, capacity, agency, optimization, or power concerns remain unresolved.

## Pause

Consequential risks or human-centered concerns are too significant to justify expansion before further review.

These categories are guides for stewardship, not automatic decisions.

# 6. Quick Meeting Template

Use this structure during a meeting:

**AI Use Case:**
What are we considering?

**Human Need:**
What real problem is being solved?

**Human Value:**
What should remain meaningfully human?

**Human Load:**
What burden should disappear?

**Allocation:**
Human-Led / AI-Led / Shared?

**Capacity Return:**
Who gets the saved capacity?

**Optimization:**
What are we actually maximizing?

**Empowerment:**
Can affected humans understand, decline, and contest?

**Power:**
Who gains functional control?

**Human Outcome:**
What do we expect to improve for humans?

**Primary Risk:**
What could go wrong?

**Decision:**
Proceed / Safeguard / Pilot / Reallocate / Redesign / Pause

---

# 7. Core Principle

The HCOS™ Quick AI Check can be summarized in one question:

> **What should AI carry so humans have greater capacity for what matters?**

And one safeguard:

> **Do not judge AI only by what it can produce. Judge it by what it allows humans to become, preserve, choose, understand, and participate in.**
