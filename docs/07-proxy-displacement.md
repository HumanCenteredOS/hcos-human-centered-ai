# HCOS™ Proxy Displacement

**Human-Centered Evaluation of When a Measure, Metric, or Operational Proxy Replaces the Human Purpose It Was Meant to Serve**

**Version:** 1.0 Draft
**Framework:** Human-Centered Operating Systems™ (HCOS™)
**Document Type:** Core Failure Mode
**Parent Architecture:** HCOS™ Human-Centered AI Architecture
**Related Construct:** HCOS™ Optimization Alignment
**Status:** Draft / Under Development

---

## 1. Purpose

HCOS™ Proxy Displacement identifies a common failure in human systems:

> **A measurable indicator created to represent a human purpose gradually becomes the thing the system actually serves.**

Most complex human purposes are difficult to measure directly.

Organizations therefore create proxies.

For example:

* learning may be represented by test scores,
* health by quality measures,
* connection by engagement,
* productivity by output,
* service quality by response time,
* safety by incident counts.

These proxies can be useful.

The problem begins when the proxy stops functioning as evidence about the human purpose and starts functioning as the purpose itself.

HCOS calls this:

## Proxy Displacement

> **Proxy Displacement occurs when a measurable indicator created to represent a human purpose gradually becomes the thing the system actually serves.**

---

# 2. The Foundational Distinction

HCOS distinguishes:

## Human Purpose

What humans actually care about.

from:

## Proxy

A measurable indicator intended to provide information about whether that purpose is being achieved.

Therefore:

> **The measure is not the meaning.**

And:

> **The metric is not the human outcome.**

---

# 3. Why Proxies Are Necessary

Proxy Displacement does not mean metrics are inherently harmful.

Human systems need ways to:

* evaluate performance,
* allocate resources,
* detect problems,
* compare outcomes,
* establish accountability,
* learn,
* and improve.

Many important human outcomes cannot be measured directly.

Therefore proxies are often necessary.

The HCOS concern is not:

**Are we measuring something?**

It is:

> **Do we still remember what the measurement is for?**

---

# 4. The Proxy Displacement Pathway

A typical pathway looks like:

**HUMAN PURPOSE**

↓

**PROXY CREATED**

↓

**PROXY BECOMES MEASURED**

↓

**MEASUREMENT BECOMES IMPORTANT**

↓

**INCENTIVES FORM AROUND THE PROXY**

↓

**RESOURCES FOLLOW THE PROXY**

↓

**BEHAVIOR ADAPTS TO THE PROXY**

↓

**TECHNOLOGY OPTIMIZES THE PROXY**

↓

**PROXY BECOMES THE PRACTICAL PURPOSE**

↓

**ORIGINAL HUMAN PURPOSE BECOMES SECONDARY**

This drift may happen gradually.

No individual actor needs to intentionally replace the original purpose.

---

# 5. A Simple Example

Suppose the human purpose is:

**Help people feel connected.**

A platform measures:

**engagement.**

Engagement becomes:

**time on platform.**

Time on platform drives:

**revenue.**

Then product teams optimize:

**attention capture.**

The pathway becomes:

**Connection**

↓

**Engagement**

↓

**Time on Platform**

↓

**Revenue**

↓

**Attention Optimization**

The organization may still describe its mission as connection.

But operationally, the proxy may now dominate.

That is Proxy Displacement.

---

# 6. Healthcare Example

Human purpose:

**Improve health.**

Proxy:

**Quality metric.**

Incentive:

**Performance score or reimbursement.**

System behavior:

**Optimize documentation, threshold attainment, or metric completion.**

Possible human outcome:

The metric improves while some broader aspects of health, access, relationship, or patient experience do not.

The problem is not necessarily the quality metric.

The problem is when:

> **Metric success becomes more important than the health outcome it was created to represent.**

---

# 7. Education Example

Human purpose:

**Help students learn.**

Proxy:

**Test scores.**

Incentive:

**Accountability, ranking, funding, evaluation.**

System behavior:

**Teaching increasingly focuses on measured content.**

Possible human outcome:

Scores rise while:

* curiosity,
* deep understanding,
* creativity,
* or broader learning

may not improve proportionally.

The test may still provide useful information.

Proxy Displacement occurs when:

**Learning becomes subordinated to score production.**

---

# 8. Workplace Example

Human purpose:

**Create valuable work sustainably.**

Proxy:

**Productivity.**

Measurement:

**Tickets closed, calls completed, units produced, hours billed, messages answered.**

Incentive:

**Performance evaluation.**

System behavior:

Workers optimize measurable output.

Possible consequences:

* lower quality,
* reduced learning,
* hidden overtime,
* burnout,
* less collaboration,
* or avoidance of difficult but important work.

The proxy may reward what is countable rather than what is valuable.

---

# 9. AI Makes Proxy Displacement More Powerful

AI can optimize a target repeatedly, quickly, and at scale.

That means Proxy Displacement may become more consequential.

If the target is slightly misaligned, AI can amplify the misalignment.

For example:

**Optimize engagement**

may become:

**Find increasingly effective ways to capture attention.**

Or:

**Reduce handling time**

may become:

**End interactions quickly even if problems remain unresolved.**

Therefore:

> **The more powerful the optimizer, the more important it is to ensure the proxy remains subordinate to the human purpose.**

---

# 10. Goodhart-Type Pressure

When a measure becomes important enough, behavior adapts to it.

Humans and technologies begin asking:

**How do we improve the score?**

rather than:

**How do we improve the underlying human outcome?**

HCOS therefore looks for:

* gaming,
* threshold targeting,
* metric manipulation,
* selective inclusion,
* documentation optimization,
* easy-case preference,
* avoidance of hard-to-measure work,
* or behavior designed primarily to improve measured performance.

These are signs that the proxy may be exerting excessive control.

---

# 11. Proxy Success Can Hide Human Failure

A dangerous condition occurs when:

**Metric Improves**

but:

**Human Outcome Worsens**

For example:

**Response Time ↓**

while:

**Problem Resolution ↓**

Or:

**Productivity ↑**

while:

**Burnout ↑**

Or:

**Engagement ↑**

while:

**Well-being ↓**

Therefore:

> **Proxy improvement should never be treated as conclusive evidence of human improvement.**

---

# 12. Proxy Failure Can Also Hide Human Success

The reverse can occur.

A human outcome may improve even when the proxy does not.

For example:

A teacher may spend more time helping a struggling student.

This could lower average throughput.

But learning may improve substantially for that student.

A clinician may spend longer with a complex patient.

Efficiency metrics may worsen.

Care quality may improve.

Therefore:

> **Human value can exist outside the metric.**

---

# 13. Measurement Boundary

Every proxy creates a boundary.

It measures something.

It leaves other things outside.

For example, a productivity metric may capture:

* volume,
* speed,
* completion.

It may not capture:

* mentoring,
* emotional labor,
* problem prevention,
* creativity,
* relationship repair,
* invisible coordination,
* or long-term capacity.

HCOS asks:

> **What important human contribution disappears outside the measurement boundary?**

---

# 14. Proxy Displacement and Hidden Human Load

A metric may improve because humans absorb unmeasured burden.

For example:

**Official Workflow Time Decreases**

because workers:

* prepare outside working hours,
* create personal tracking systems,
* fix errors later,
* or carry more cognitive load.

The proxy says:

**Efficiency improved.**

The human experience may say:

**The burden moved somewhere invisible.**

Therefore Proxy Displacement should be evaluated with Externalized Human Load.

---

# 15. Proxy Displacement and Capacity Return

Suppose AI improves productivity.

The proxy shows:

**More output per employee.**

But if all capacity gains are reinvested into more work, the human may receive no capacity return.

The metric improves.

Human sustainability does not.

Therefore:

> **Productivity is not a complete measure of human benefit.**

---

# 16. Proxy Displacement and Human Capacity Protection

A system may optimize task completion while weakening underlying human skill.

For example:

**Student Completion Rate ↑**

while:

**Independent Reasoning ↓**

or:

**Decision Speed ↑**

while:

**Human Judgment Capacity ↓**

This matters because the proxy may show success even while long-term human capability erodes.

---

# 17. Proxy Displacement and Meaningful Technological Empowerment

A system may measure:

**AI Adoption**

and conclude:

**People are empowered by AI.**

But adoption may occur because:

* the tool is mandatory,
* alternatives were removed,
* refusal is costly,
* or people have no meaningful control.

Therefore:

> **Use is not the same as empowerment.**

A better evaluation would include:

* access,
* understanding,
* benefit,
* agency,
* ability to decline,
* and ability to contest.

---

# 18. Proxy Displacement and Power

Metrics are not neutral in their effects.

The actor who chooses the proxy influences what becomes visible and valuable.

Ask:

* Who chose the metric?
* Who benefits when it improves?
* Who is judged by it?
* Who bears the consequences?
* Who can challenge it?
* Whose experience is excluded?

Therefore:

> **Choosing a proxy is also an exercise of power.**

---

# 19. Proxy Ownership

HCOS asks:

> **Who owns the definition of success?**

If only the most powerful actor defines success, important human outcomes may disappear.

For example:

An organization may define successful automation as:

**Reduced cost.**

Workers may define it as:

**Reduced burden.**

Customers may define it as:

**Better service.**

All three matter.

Proxy design should therefore consider multiple stakeholders.

---

# 20. Proxy Compression

Complex human purposes are often compressed into one number.

For example:

**Patient Experience = Survey Score**

or:

**Employee Performance = Productivity Metric**

or:

**Learning = Test Score**

HCOS calls this:

## Proxy Compression

> **Proxy Compression occurs when a multidimensional human outcome is represented too narrowly by a limited measurement.**

The narrower the proxy, the greater the risk that important human dimensions disappear.

---

# 21. Proxy Stacking

Organizations may create multiple proxies.

This can improve balance.

But it can also create:

## Proxy Stacking

A system becomes increasingly complex as multiple measures accumulate.

Humans then spend substantial effort:

* tracking metrics,
* documenting compliance,
* reconciling measures,
* and optimizing scores.

Eventually, the measurement system itself becomes a source of Externalized Human Load.

Therefore:

> **Adding more metrics is not always the solution to a bad metric.**

---

# 22. Proxy Lag

Some human outcomes take time.

Metrics may be available much sooner.

For example:

A platform can measure clicks immediately.

Meaningful relationship effects may take months or years.

A school can measure a test score this semester.

Long-term learning may emerge later.

This creates:

## Proxy Lag

> **Proxy Lag occurs when short-term measurable indicators dominate decision-making because the deeper human outcomes appear more slowly.**

HCOS therefore asks:

**Are we optimizing what appears fastest rather than what matters most?**

---

# 23. Proxy Visibility Bias

What is easy to measure often receives more attention.

This can create:

## Proxy Visibility Bias

> **Visible, measurable outcomes may receive disproportionate weight compared with important but less measurable human outcomes.**

Examples of less visible outcomes may include:

* trust,
* dignity,
* confidence,
* belonging,
* cognitive burden,
* relational quality,
* psychological safety,
* creativity,
* and meaning.

These should not be treated as unimportant merely because measurement is harder.

---

# 24. Proxy Multiplication Through AI

AI can create enormous quantities of metrics.

Organizations may increasingly measure:

* behavior,
* sentiment,
* performance,
* attention,
* risk,
* prediction,
* compliance,
* productivity,
* communication,
* and interaction.

More measurement can create insight.

It can also create more opportunities for Proxy Displacement.

Therefore:

> **Measurement capability should not automatically become measurement authority.**

The ability to quantify something does not prove that the number should govern decisions.

---

# 25. The Metric-to-Control Path

Proxy Displacement can eventually become a control problem.

The pathway may look like:

**Human Purpose**

↓

**Proxy**

↓

**Target**

↓

**Performance Expectation**

↓

**Monitoring**

↓

**Comparison**

↓

**Pressure**

↓

**Behavioral Control**

The system may begin using the metric not merely to learn but to compel.

HCOS therefore distinguishes:

## Measurement for Learning

from:

## Measurement for Control

Both may sometimes be legitimate.

But their effects are different.

---

# 26. Learning-Oriented Metrics

A human-centered metric should help answer:

**What can we learn?**

rather than only:

**Who failed?**

Learning-oriented measurement may:

* identify patterns,
* reveal barriers,
* surface inequity,
* show where support is needed,
* detect system failure,
* or inform redesign.

This reduces the risk that humans hide problems to protect their scores.

---

# 27. Proxy Displacement Warning Signs

Watch for statements such as:

* "The numbers are good, so the program is working."
* "That doesn't count because we don't measure it."
* "We need to improve the metric."
* "Just make sure the score goes up."
* "We can't change that because it hurts performance."
* "The AI says the target was met."
* "Everyone is using it, so they must like it."
* "Productivity increased, so employees benefited."

These statements may not always be wrong.

But they should trigger the question:

> **What happened to the underlying human purpose?**

---

# 28. Proxy Displacement Diagnostic

Ask:

### Human Purpose

What human outcome was the proxy created to represent?

### Proxy

What are we actually measuring?

### Validity

How strongly does the proxy correlate with the human purpose?

### Missing Dimensions

What important parts of the purpose are not represented?

### Incentive

What behavior does the proxy reward?

### Gaming

How could humans or AI improve the proxy without improving the purpose?

### Power

Who controls the metric?

### Burden

What work does measurement itself create?

### Human Outcome

Can the proxy improve while humans become worse off?

### Time

Does the proxy capture short-term or long-term outcomes?

### Control

Is the metric being used for learning, governance, punishment, or control?

### Revision

What evidence would cause us to change or abandon the proxy?

# 30. Proxy Displacement Failure Modes

## Metric Substitution

The metric is treated as equivalent to the human purpose.

## Gaming

Performance improves through behavior that does not improve the underlying outcome.

## Proxy Compression

A complex human outcome is represented too narrowly.

## Proxy Stacking

Multiple metrics create administrative complexity and new human load.

## Proxy Lag

Short-term metrics dominate long-term human outcomes.

## Visibility Bias

Easy-to-measure outcomes crowd out important but less measurable outcomes.

## Measurement-Control Drift

A learning tool becomes primarily a mechanism of control.

## AI Optimization Amplification

AI rapidly increases performance on a misaligned proxy.

---

# 31. Safeguards Against Proxy Displacement

HCOS recommends:

### Keep the Human Purpose Visible

Write it explicitly.

Do not allow it to disappear behind technical metrics.

### Use Multiple Forms of Evidence

Combine:

* quantitative data,
* qualitative feedback,
* lived experience,
* human outcomes,
* and contextual information.

### Measure the Cost of the Metric

Ask what burden measurement creates.

### Look for Counter-Metrics

If productivity increases, also examine:

* quality,
* capacity,
* load,
* sustainability,
* and human outcomes.

### Allow Humans to Challenge the Proxy

People affected by the metric should be able to identify where it fails.

### Review Incentives

Ask what behavior the proxy encourages.

### Reassess Regularly

A proxy that was useful yesterday may become misleading tomorrow.

# 32. Quick Proxy Displacement Test

Ask:

1. **What human purpose are we trying to serve?**
2. **What proxy are we using?**
3. **What does the proxy actually measure?**
4. **What important human outcomes does it miss?**
5. **What behaviors does it reward?**
6. **Could someone improve the metric without improving the human outcome?**
7. **Could the metric improve while humans become worse off?**
8. **Who chose the proxy?**
9. **Who is judged by it?**
10. **What burden does measuring it create?**
11. **Is AI amplifying optimization toward the proxy?**
12. **Is the metric being used to learn or primarily to control?**
13. **Do affected humans have a way to challenge it?**
14. **Are we still discussing the original human purpose?**
15. **Would we recognize success if it occurred outside the metric?**

Then ask:

> **Are we using the measurement to understand the purpose—or have we started serving the measurement itself?**

# 33. Core Principles

### Purpose Principle

> **The proxy should remain subordinate to the human purpose.**

### Measurement Principle

> **The measure is not the meaning.**

### Evidence Principle

> **A proxy provides evidence about a human outcome; it does not define the outcome.**

### Visibility Principle

> **What is difficult to measure is not automatically less important.**

### Incentive Principle

> **Once a proxy becomes consequential, behavior will adapt to it.**

### AI Principle

> **AI can optimize a bad proxy extremely well.**

### Power Principle

> **Those who define the metric exercise significant governance power.**

### Learning Principle

> **Metrics should help systems learn, not merely make humans easier to rank or control.**

---

# 34. Canonical HCOS™ Proxy Displacement Proposition

> **A human-centered system should use metrics as evidence about human purposes without allowing those metrics to replace, compress, or redefine the purposes they were created to represent.**

More simply:

> **Do not mistake the score for the thing that matters.**
