**A Human-Centered Evaluation of AI, Automation, Capacity, Power, and Human Outcomes**

**Version:** 1.0 Draft

**Instrument Type:** Assessment and Stewardship Tool
**Parent Architecture:** HCOS™ Human-Centered AI Architecture
**Status:** Draft / Under Development

---

## 1. Purpose

The HCOS™ Human-Centered AI Assessment helps individuals, teams, organizations, institutions, designers, leaders, and communities evaluate whether an AI-enabled system is actually increasing human value.

The assessment does not ask only:

> **Does the AI work?**

It also asks:

> **What does the AI change for the humans involved?**

The instrument evaluates:

* human need,
* human value,
* externalized human load,
* human capacity,
* intelligence allocation,
* capacity return,
* optimization alignment,
* proxy displacement,
* technological empowerment,
* power,
* human outcomes,
* repair,
* and learning.

The purpose is not to determine whether AI is inherently good or bad.

The purpose is to determine:

> **What should remain human, what should AI carry, who receives the benefit, and what actually happens to humans as a result?**

---

# 2. When to Use This Assessment

Use this instrument when:

* considering a new AI tool,
* redesigning a workflow,
* automating human work,
* evaluating an existing AI deployment,
* responding to concerns about AI,
* reviewing workforce impact,
* examining AI governance,
* assessing a high-stakes automated decision system,
* or determining whether technology is genuinely increasing human capacity.

It can be used:

**Before Deployment**

to guide design.

**During Implementation**

to detect emerging problems.

**After Deployment**

to evaluate real-world outcomes.

---

# 3. Assessment Instructions

For each section:

1. Describe what is currently known.
2. Identify what remains uncertain.
3. Include perspectives from affected humans.
4. Distinguish technical assumptions from human outcomes.
5. Document evidence where available.
6. Do not treat a single metric as sufficient proof.
7. Identify where power, burden, risk, and benefit are located.
8. Reassess when new evidence appears.

The assessment should support judgment.

It should not replace judgment.

# 8. Section E — Appropriate Intelligence Allocation

> **Who should carry what?**

* relationship,

* or desired participation

### AI-Led

Use when the work is primarily:

* repetitive,
* computational,

* memory,
* analysis,

# 21. Overall Classification

The assessment may conclude that the AI system is currently:

## Human-Centered and Well-Aligned

Human need, value, capacity, allocation, empowerment, and outcomes are substantially aligned.

## Human-Centered With Safeguards Needed

The use case is valuable, but additional protections, oversight, or redesign are needed.

## Technically Effective but Humanly Misaligned

The system performs well but creates important human burden, agency loss, capacity erosion, power imbalance, or poor outcomes.

## Poorly Allocated

The AI is carrying something humans should meaningfully retain, or humans remain burdened with work technology should carry.

## Capacity-Capturing

The technology produces efficiency without meaningful human capacity return.

## Proxy-Displaced

Operational metrics or optimization targets have begun replacing the original human purpose.

## Disempowering

Humans are substantially affected by the technology without meaningful understanding, control, refusal, contestability, or recourse.

## Requires Redesign Before Expansion

Material human-centered concerns remain unresolved.

These classifications are diagnostic rather than punitive.

Their purpose is to guide learning and redesign.

# 22. Canonical Assessment Principle

> **An AI system should not be considered human-centered merely because it is useful, efficient, accurate, popular, or technically successful. Human-centered success requires that technological capability be allocated and governed in ways that reduce unnecessary human burden while protecting or increasing dignity, autonomy, agency, capacity, connection, safety, meaning, participation, and human flourishing.**

And in the simplest form:

> **What should remain human, what should AI carry, who receives the benefit, and what happened to the human?**
